# EAGLE-3 batch-size headroom sweep — findings

**Setup:** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct + yuhuili/EAGLE3-LLaMA3.1-Instruct-8B head, vLLM
0.23 V1 on Modal H100, greedy (temp=0), max_tokens=64, FA3 attention. B=1 row
re-used from `results/eagle3_8b/fixedK_by_workload.csv` (the deliverable trace);
B=8/32/64 from `vllm_eagle3_b{N}_llama8b*.json` in this directory.

**Question (from Yash's email, 2026-06-26):** does the per-request oracle gap
widen as batch grows? If yes, per-step adaptive K has real headroom in the
batched serving regime even though B=1 ceiling is only +2%.

## Best K vs batch (gap_vs_batch.csv)

| workload | B=1 best K | B=8 best K | B=32 best K | B=64 best K |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| humaneval | 2 (1.64x) | 1 (1.29x) | 1 (1.11x) | 1 (1.08x) |
| gsm8k     | 3 (1.65x) | 2 (1.38x) | 1 (1.21x) | 1 (1.15x) |
| mt_bench  | 2 (1.36x) | 2 (1.20x) | 1 (1.10x) | 1 (1.10x) |

## Gap vs fixed-K=1 (the controller's headroom)

| workload | B=1 | B=8 | B=32 | B=64 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| humaneval | +0.199 | +0.000 | +0.000 | +0.000 |
| gsm8k     | +0.216 | +0.077 | +0.000 | +0.000 |
| mt_bench  | +0.070 | +0.063 | +0.000 | +0.000 |

At B>=32, K=1 is the unanimous best across all three workloads, and K>=2 is
strictly worse. There is nothing for a per-step or per-request controller to do.

## Read

**Yash's prediction was directionally right but sharper than expected.** He
thought the *oracle gap would widen* with batch. What actually happens is that
the *headroom collapses entirely*: as B grows, verification cost dominates,
optimal K converges to 1 across all workloads, and K>=2 goes net-negative.

- B=1: per-request oracle ceiling = +2.0% (from `results/eagle3_8b/policies.csv`).
- B=8: per-workload best K disagrees (1 vs 2 vs 2), gap up to +6-8%. Still small.
- B=32: best K = 1 uniformly. Gap = 0. K=3 break-even; K=4 hurts.
- B=64: best K = 1 uniformly. Gap = 0. K=3 hurts on humaneval; K=4 hurts on all.

Spec still helps (+15% mean at B=32, +11% at B=64), but the *adaptive* part of
draft-length adaptive K is dead in this regime — there's no K to adapt TO above
the minimum-draft K=1.

## What this means for the project

1. **Per-step adaptive K is not the workshop story at high batch.** The per-step
   oracle gap measured at B=1 (+24.9%, signal-limited to +0.3% with entropy)
   is the only place residual headroom lived; the batched regime doesn't
   resurrect it.
2. **The clean publishable claim shifts to:** "On modern speculative substrates
   (EAGLE-3) the optimal draft length is K=1 across batch sizes B>=32, so
   per-step adaptive K is a no-op in serving — adaptive *decisions* should
   instead be about whether to speculate at all, not how deep to draft."
3. The B=64 row shows spec yields only +11% over no-spec — the *speculate vs
   not* decision (at high B) is where the next interesting controller question
   lives. EAGLE-3 K=1 is still net-positive but the margin is small enough
   that draft-token contention with prefill could flip it on real traces.
